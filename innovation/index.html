02 / THE APPROACH

Discover. Test.
Improve.

A small, focused experiment is easier to evaluate than a sweeping technology project. The proposed approach keeps the work visible and the decisions yours.

  1. 01

    Understand the work

    Walk through one workflow: who does what, where information goes, and what keeps getting in the way. Agree what a useful improvement would look like.

    DiscoverA clear problem and a practical starting point.

  2. 02

    Try a small change

    Test a process change, an existing tool, or a limited AI prototype. Set boundaries for the information used and build in human review where it matters.

    TestA bounded experiment you can inspect.

  3. 03

    Keep what helps

    Compare the result with the starting point: effort, quality, cost, and ease of use. Improve it, document it, or stop if the value does not justify the investment.

    ImproveEvidence for the next decision.

A FEW PRACTICAL QUESTIONS

Before adding
another tool.

Do I need AI or new software?

No. The starting point is the problem. A process change, a checklist, or better use of a tool you already have may be enough. AI is an option to evaluate when it could help.

What would a first conversation cover?

One task that takes too much effort: how it works today, where it gets stuck, and what a better outcome would mean. You can describe it without sharing customer records or confidential documents.

Would AI make decisions for my business?

The proposed approach keeps you responsible for business decisions. Any pilot would define what a tool can do, what needs a person’s review, and how its output will be checked.
